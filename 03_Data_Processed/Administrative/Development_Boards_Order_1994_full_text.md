# The Development Boards for Vidarbha, Marathwada and the Rest of Maharashtra Order, 1994

**Issued under:** Article 371(2) of the Constitution of India, via the State of Maharashtra
(Special Responsibility of Governor for Vidarbha, Marathwada and the Rest of Maharashtra) Order,
1994

**Notification:** No. GS/G/94/DB/432, dated 30 April 1994
**Published/commenced:** 30 April 1994
**Issuing authority:** P. C. Alexander, Governor of Maharashtra

### 1. Short title and commencement
(1) This order may be called the Development Boards for Vidarbha, Marathwada and the Rest of
Maharashtra Order, 1994.
(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Governor may, by an order in the Official
Gazette, appoint.
(3) It shall remain in force up to and inclusive of such date as the State of Maharashtra
(Special Responsibility of Governor for Vidarbha, Marathwada and the Rest of Maharashtra) Order,
1994 remains in force.

### 2. Establishment of Development Boards
The Governor of Maharashtra shall, by order made in this behalf, constitute a separate
Development Board, one each for Vidarbha, Marathwada and the rest of Maharashtra area in the
State. These Boards shall be known respectively as:
(a) The Vidarbha Development Board;
(b) The Marathwada Development Board;
(c) The Development Board for the Rest of Maharashtra.

### 3. Areas of Development Boards
(a) **The Vidarbha Development Board** shall cover the areas of the **Nagpur and Amravati
Revenue Divisions**.
(b) The Marathwada Development Board shall cover the areas of the Aurangabad (Chhatrapati
Sambhajinagar) Revenue Division.
(c) The Development Board for the Rest of Maharashtra shall cover the areas of the Konkan, Pune
and Nashik Revenue Divisions.

### 4. Composition of Development Boards
(1) Each Development Board shall consist of members not exceeding 7, including the Chairman, all
appointed by the Governor:
(a) One member of the Maharashtra State Legislature from the area of the respective Development
Board;
(b) One member of a local authority from the area of the respective Development Board;
(c) Three experts from among persons who — (i) have special knowledge of the planning process,
finances and accounts of Government; or (ii) have had wide experience in financial matters and
administration; or (iii) have special knowledge in different fields like irrigation, public
health, public works, industries, agriculture, education or employment;
(d) A Commissioner of the Revenue Division from the area of the respective Development Board;
(e) An officer of the State Government not below the rank of Additional Commissioner of a
Revenue Division from the respective Development Board.
(2) The officer referred to in sub-clause (e) of clause (1) shall be Member Secretary of each
respective Development Board.

### 5. Term of office and allowances
(1) The term of office of the Chairman and members shall be as the Governor specifies while
appointing them. The Governor may terminate or reconstitute the Board before expiry of term.
(2) Members (other than experts under 4(1)(c)) cease to be members as soon as they cease to
belong to the category from which they were appointed.
(3) Any member, including the Chairman (other than those under 4(1)(d) and (e)), may resign in
writing to the Governor.
(4) The Chairman and members shall be paid fees/allowances for attending meetings and performing
functions; members under 4(1)(e) shall additionally be paid such honorarium as the Governor may
fix.

### 6. Functions of the Development Board
The Development Board shall, from time to time:
(a) ascertain relative levels of development in different sectors in relation to its area, on the
basis of appropriate indicators, having regard to the levels of development in the State as a
whole;
(b) assess the impact of various development efforts in removing backlog and achieving overall
development within its area;
(c) suggest the levels of development expenditure over the area of the Development Board during a
plan period, including the annual plan; and
(d) prepare an annual report on its working and send it, as far as practicable within three
months after the end of every financial year, to the Governor for placing before the Maharashtra
State Legislature.

### 7. Allocation of funds for development expenditure
(1) The Governor shall ensure equitable allocation of funds for development expenditure over the
areas of the Development Boards, subject to the requirements of the State as a whole.
(2) In ensuring equitable allocation, the Governor may (a) consider recommendations, if any, of
the Development Board, and (b) seek advice from any person or body of persons where necessary.

### 8. Allocation of funds to be reflected in Annual Financial Statements
The allocation of funds/outlays made by the Governor shall be reflected in the Annual Financial
Statement placed before the State Legislature. Development activities shall be carried out by the
State Government, and funds so allocated **shall be non-divertible from the area of one Board to
that of another Board**, provided that:
(a) reappropriation may be made in conformity with budgetary rules within the area of a Board;
(b) prevailing norms shall be adhered to in implementation; and
(c) respective administrative departments shall continue to exercise administrative and technical
supervision and control over the developmental activities.

### 9. Directives by the Governor
The Governor may, by order, from time to time give directions to a Development Board regarding
its functioning.

### 10. Arrangements for education, training and employment
The Governor shall ensure equitable arrangements providing adequate facilities for technical
education and vocational training, and adequate opportunities for employment in services under
the control of the State Government, in respect of the area of each Development Board — subject
to the requirements of the State as a whole — and give suitable directions to the State Government
for that purpose.

### 11. Rules
The Governor may, by order, make rules for the proper transaction of business relating to
functions under Article 371(2) and for the effective implementation of this Order.

## Why this matters for THE COTTON LINE

This is the operative legal instrument behind the Article 371(2) special-responsibility clause
cited in this paper's Introduction — the modern institutional counterpart to the 1853 Ryotwari/
Malguzari administrative split this study tests. Key points relevant to this project:

- The **non-divertibility clause (Clause 8)** is the legal mechanism meant to stop funds earmarked
  for Vidarbha being spent elsewhere — relevant background for the paper's framing of Vidarbha's
  modern-day agrarian-distress governance (Section 1, Introduction).
- The Board only *recommends* — actual allocation power stays with the Governor (Clause 7), a useful
  "paper safeguard vs. practice" contrast alongside this project's own institutional-persistence
  argument.
- The Vidarbha Development Board's jurisdiction (Nagpur + Amravati Revenue Divisions, Clause 3)
  matches the same 11-district Vidarbha study area used throughout this project, confirming the
  modern administrative geography that this paper's boundary construction (Section 3.3) relies on.
